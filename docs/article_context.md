# Article Context and Dataset Mapping

## Associated Article

Li Q, Qi K, Wu S, Shen A, Zhao L. High-throughput screening reveals
paeoniflorin's efficacy against Apoc2-deficient hypertriglyceridemia via
HNF4A/PPARA/LDLR. *Biochemical Pharmacology*. 2025;242:117351.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bcp.2025.117351

## Study Purpose

The study addresses hypertriglyceridemia (HTG), especially severe or refractory
forms where current treatment options remain limited. The authors established an
`apoc2`-deficient zebrafish model and used it as a phenotypic screening platform
to identify small-molecule therapeutic candidates.

## Experimental Model

- Species: zebrafish (*Danio rerio*)
- Strain: wildtype AB strain background
- Disease model: `apoc2` deficiency
- Model construction:
  - morpholino knockdown was used for initial phenotype validation
  - stable `apoc2` knockout line was generated by CRISPR/Cas9
  - the reported knockout targeted exon 3 and produced a 17-nucleotide
    frameshift deletion
- Phenotype:
  - increased neutral lipid accumulation in larvae
  - elevated whole-larva TG and TC
  - turbid/yellowish adult plasma and elevated plasma TG
- Animal ethics approval: ZJU20250466

## Screening Design From the Article

- Compound library: 351 natural small molecules
- Primary screening concentration: 100 uM
- Treatment window: 48 hpf to 6 dpf
- Screening format: larvae in 96-well plates for primary screening
- Readout: BODIPY 505/515 fluorescence signal in the caudal vessel at 6 dpf
- Positive control: gemfibrozil (GEMF), 2.5 uM
- Hit selection benchmark: compounds outperforming GEMF in reducing vascular
  lipid deposition
- Primary screen result: 6 candidate compounds, 1.71% hit rate
- Secondary screen: concentration-gradient validation
- Key validated hit: paeoniflorin (PAE)

## Main Findings Relevant to the Dataset

- PAE reduced BODIPY-positive vascular lipid deposition in `apoc2` mutant larvae.
- PAE reduced whole-body TG and TC.
- Oil Red O staining confirmed reduced neutral lipid deposition after PAE
  treatment.
- Lipidomics suggested enhanced triglyceride catabolism, beta-oxidation, and
  lipophagy.
- Mechanistic assays supported an HNF4A/PPARA/LDLR axis.
